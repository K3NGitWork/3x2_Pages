Support

Encode does one thing: it makes videos smaller and leaves everything else as it is. Most questions are answered below.

Getting started

  • Install: open the DMG and drag Encode to Applications. It needs macOS 26 and a Mac with Apple silicon.
  • Add videos: drop videos or whole folders on the window or on Encode in the Dock, or click +.
  • Encode: click Start. Encode works through the list one video at a time; Pause, Resume and Stop are in the toolbar.
  • Settings (⌘,): where the new files go, the preset (Smaller, Balanced, Best) and Fast hardware mode.

Frequently asked questions

Will my video look worse?

With the Balanced preset, most videos show no visible difference. For footage you want to keep at its best, choose Best in Settings › Quality. The original is never changed or deleted.

Why .mkv?

MKV holds every audio track, subtitle and chapter of the original as it is. It plays in IINA, VLC, Infuse and most TVs and media players. QuickTime Player doesn’t open it.

How much smaller will it get?

Typically 40 to 60%. It depends on the video: phone and camera footage shrinks a lot, videos that are already compressed hard shrink less. If the new file isn’t smaller, Encode deletes it and keeps the original (“Not smaller · kept the original”).

What is fast hardware mode?

It encodes with the Mac’s media engine instead of x265. That is many times faster, but the files come out larger for the same quality. It is off by default.

Which formats can it read?

Almost any video: MP4, MOV, MKV, AVI, WebM, MTS and M2TS, MPEG, WMV and more. Videos that are already H.265 or AV1 are kept as they are (“Already H.265 · kept the original”): encoding them again would only lose quality. Interlaced video, from old camcorders and TV recordings, isn’t supported yet (“Can’t encode interlaced video”).

Can I encode several videos at once?

Add as many as you like: drop files or whole folders, or use +. Encode works through them one at a time, top to bottom, which is the fastest way on a Mac. Drag waiting rows to change the order.

Where is the new file?

Next to the original, with the same name and .mkv. If the original is already an .mkv, the new one goes into a folder named encode beside it. To collect them somewhere else, choose a folder in Settings › Output › Save to. Encode never overwrites a file: if the name is taken, it adds a number. Click a finished row to show the file in Finder.

Is it safe to close the lid?

Encode keeps your Mac awake while it works, as long as it stays open. Closing the lid of a MacBook puts it to sleep anyway (unless it is connected to power and a display); encoding then stops and carries on when the Mac wakes up. Pause and Resume work the same way at any time.

Why is x265 GPL, and where is the source?

Encode encodes with x265 and reads and writes the files with FFmpeg. Both are free software under the GNU General Public License, and Encode includes them as separate programs, built from their unmodified source. The exact source of both is on the Licenses page.

Messages

  • “Not smaller · kept the original”: the new file came out as large as the original or larger, so Encode deleted it. The video was already compressed well.
  • “Already H.265 · kept the original” (or AV1): the video already uses a codec at least as efficient. Nothing to gain.
  • “Can’t read the video track”: the file is damaged, isn’t a video, or uses a format FFmpeg can’t read.
  • “Can’t encode interlaced video”: old camcorder and TV recordings store half-pictures; Encode would encode them wrong, so it doesn’t.
  • “Not enough space on …”: free some space on that disk, or choose another folder in Settings › Output, then click Retry.
  • “Moved or deleted”: the original wasn’t there any more when its turn came (for example, an external drive was unplugged).

Write to hello3x2@outlook.de with your macOS version and, if possible, the video’s file name and where it came from (camera, phone, download).
